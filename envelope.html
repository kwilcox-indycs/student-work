Building Envelope

Think about what you wear on a cold, rainy day. You probably put on a jacket to stay warm, a hood to stay dry, and maybe a scarf to block the wind. A house has its own "outfit" too — and we call it the building envelope.

The envelope is everything that separates the inside of a house from the weather outside: the walls, the roof, the floor, the windows, and the doors. All these parts work together as one big shell.

What does the envelope do?

The envelope has three big jobs:

  • Keep heat in or out. It traps warm air inside in winter and blocks hot air in summer.
  • Keep water out. Rain, snow, and groundwater all want to get in. The envelope blocks them.
  • Control air. Wind shouldn't blow through the walls. The envelope keeps inside air inside and outside air outside.

The four control layers

Builders think about four invisible "layers" inside a wall. Each one has a different job:

  1. Water control — sheds rain off the building
  2. Air control — stops air from leaking through
  3. Vapor control — manages moisture floating in the air
  4. Thermal control — that's insulation, which slows heat

If any of these layers has a hole, the whole envelope works less well. That's why builders are so careful when they put a house together!

Did you know?

A small gap around a window can leak as much air as a hole the size of a soccer ball in your wall. Tiny cracks really add up!

Words to know

Envelope
The outer "shell" of a house — walls, roof, floor, windows, and doors.
Control layer
A part of the wall that does one specific job, like blocking water or air.
Air leak
A spot where air sneaks in or out where it shouldn't.
