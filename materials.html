Materials & Assemblies

Houses aren't made of just one material. They're built in layers, like a sandwich. We call these layers an assembly.

A typical wall, from outside to inside

  1. Siding — the outer skin (wood, vinyl, brick, or stucco)
  2. House wrap — a thin sheet that blocks water and air
  3. Sheathing — usually plywood, nailed to the framing for strength
  4. Framing — the wooden "skeleton" (those 2×4s you see in construction)
  5. Insulation — fills the spaces between the framing
  6. Drywall — the smooth interior wall you paint

Common building materials

  • Wood — strong, light, and grows back. The most common framing material in homes.
  • Concrete — heavy and rock-solid. Used for foundations and slabs.
  • Steel — super strong. Used in big buildings and some house parts.
  • Brick and stone — long-lasting and beautiful, but heavy and expensive.

Different climates, different houses

A house in Arizona doesn't look or work the same as a house in Minnesota. Hot, dry places need different walls than cold, snowy places. Builders pick materials based on the local climate, the weather, and even how strong the wind blows.

Drying potential

Even with the best design, walls sometimes get wet. A good wall can dry out when that happens. A wall that traps water inside it will rot, even if it looks perfectly fine from the outside. The ability to dry is just as important as keeping water out.

Did you know?

Some houses in Japan and Europe are over 1,000 years old! The secret? They were built with materials that could dry out, and people have taken care of them for many generations.

Words to know

Assembly
A group of layers working together — like a wall or a roof.
Framing
The skeleton of a house, usually made of wood.
Sheathing
Flat boards (often plywood) nailed to the framing for strength.
Climate
The usual weather of a place over many years.
