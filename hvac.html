HVAC & Comfort

HVAC stands for Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning. If the building envelope is the house's jacket, the HVAC system is its lungs — it warms you up, cools you down, and brings in fresh air to breathe.

Heating

In winter, a furnace, heat pump, or boiler warms air or water and sends it through ducts or pipes to every room. That's why a vent in your bedroom blows warm air on a cold morning.

Cooling

In summer, an air conditioner pulls heat out of the inside air and pushes it outside. It might feel like cold air is being made, but really, heat is being removed. Cool, right?

Ventilation

Houses also need fresh air. Without it, smells, moisture, and even chemicals from cleaning products can build up inside. Modern homes use fans and special vents to swap stale air for fresh air without losing too much heat.

Comfort is more than temperature

You don't just feel "the temperature." You also feel:

  • Humidity — how much moisture is in the air. Too much feels sticky; too little feels dry.
  • Air movement — a small breeze can make you feel cooler.
  • Surface temperatures — a cold window can chill you even if the room is warm.

Did you know?

A heating system that's too big can actually make a house less comfortable. It heats fast and shuts off, leaving rooms damp and uneven. Bigger isn't always better!

Words to know

Furnace
A heater that warms up air and blows it through the house.
Duct
A tube — usually metal — that carries air around the house.
Humidity
How much water vapor is floating around in the air.
Heat pump
A clever machine that moves heat from one place to another. It can heat and cool.
