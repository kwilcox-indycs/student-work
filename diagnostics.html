Diagnostics & Maintenance

How do we know if a house is healthy? Just like a doctor uses tools to check on you, building scientists use tools to check on houses. They look for hidden problems before those problems get expensive.

Cool tools they use

  • Blower door — a giant fan stuck in a doorway. It sucks air out of the house, and any place that whistles or feels drafty is a leak!
  • Thermal camera — shows hot and cold spots in different colors. Cold streaks on a wall? Probably missing insulation.
  • Moisture meter — a probe that detects hidden water inside walls and floors.
  • Smoke pencil — a small tool that puffs out smoke to show how air is moving.

Maintenance: the boring but important stuff

Even a perfectly built house needs care. Houses need:

  • Clean gutters so water flows away
  • New air filters every few months
  • Fresh caulk around windows, sinks, and bathtubs
  • A coat of paint on wood every several years
  • Yearly check-ups of the heating and cooling system

Why it all matters

Tiny problems become big problems when they're ignored. A small drip can rot a wall. A clogged gutter can flood a basement. A dirty filter can break a furnace. Catching problems early saves time, money, and a whole lot of trouble.

Did you know?

A house that gets regular check-ups can last more than 100 years. A neglected house can fall apart in 30. The difference is just paying attention!

Words to know

Blower door
A big fan used to find air leaks in a house.
Maintenance
The regular care that keeps something working well.
Caulk
A squishy sealant used to fill small cracks and gaps.
Filter
A part that catches dust and dirt as air moves through it.
