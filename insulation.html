Insulation

Insulation is like a fluffy blanket wrapped around your house. Here's the surprising part: insulation doesn't make heat. It just slows heat down.

Why heat moves

Heat is always trying to even things out. If it's warm inside and cold outside, the heat tries to escape. If it's hot outside and cool inside, heat tries to sneak in. Insulation puts up a roadblock either way.

R-value: the magic number

Every kind of insulation has a number called an R-value. The higher the R-value, the better it slows heat down. A thin material might have a low R-value, while a thick fluffy material might have a really high one.

Types of insulation

  • Fiberglass batts — pink or yellow rolls made of glass fibers. They look like cotton candy!
  • Cellulose — recycled paper that gets blown into walls and attics.
  • Spray foam — a liquid that puffs up into a hard foam, sealing every crack.
  • Mineral wool — made from melted rock spun into fibers. It even resists fire.

Why installation matters

Even amazing insulation works poorly if it's stuffed in the wrong way. Gaps, squished spots, and missing pieces let heat sneak through. Insulation is only as good as how carefully it's put in.

Did you know?

A house with no insulation can lose more than half its heat through the attic alone! Heat rises, and an uninsulated attic acts like a giant chimney.

Words to know

R-value
A number that measures how well a material slows heat. Higher = better.
Batts
Pre-cut rolls or panels of insulation, made to fit between wall studs.
Thermal
Anything related to heat.
